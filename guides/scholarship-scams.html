How to spot a scholarship scam

Honhaar Editorial · Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Every admission season, fake “scholarship officers” message students promising guaranteed money in exchange for a small fee. The rule that protects you every time: real scholarships pay you — you never pay them.

Seven red flags

  1. An “application”, “processing” or “registration” fee. Legitimate government and foundation scholarships are free to apply for.
  2. Guaranteed selection. “100% selection” or “approved, just confirm” are lies. Selection is competitive.
  3. Requests for OTPs, PINs, UPI approvals or card details. No scholarship needs your OTP. Approving a “collect” request sends money out of your account.
  4. Unofficial contact. Messages from personal WhatsApp numbers or free email addresses claiming to be a ministry or university.
  5. Look-alike websites. Government portals use official domains such as .gov.in, .nic.in or .gov.pk. Check the address bar character by character.
  6. Pressure and urgency. “Last 2 hours — pay now or lose your seat.” Real deadlines are published weeks in advance.
  7. “You won” without applying. You cannot win a scholarship you never applied for.

How to verify any scholarship in five minutes

  1. Search the scholarship name plus the provider’s name, and open the provider’s official website yourself — never through a link in a message.
  2. Find the scholarship on that official site. If it isn’t there, treat it as fake.
  3. Check the contact details on the official page and compare them with the message you received.
  4. Ask your school or college scholarship cell; for government schemes, the institute nodal officer can confirm.
  5. Search the exact phone number or email with the word “scam”.
Honhaar will never ask you for money, OTPs or bank details. If someone claims to be from Honhaar and asks for payment, it is not us.

Agents and consultants

Some counsellors and study-abroad consultants offer genuine, paid guidance. That is legal — but nobody can guarantee a scholarship, and you should never pay anyone to “secure” one. Ask for a written scope of service, check reviews from multiple sources, and keep all payments traceable.

If you’ve been scammed

  • Call your bank immediately to block cards or UPI and dispute the transaction.
  • In India, report cyber fraud on the national cybercrime helpline 1930 or at cybercrime.gov.in.
  • In Pakistan, report to the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) through its official complaint channels.
  • Change passwords for any account you shared, and warn your classmates — scammers often target whole batches.

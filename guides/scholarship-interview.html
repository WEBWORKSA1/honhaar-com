Scholarship interviews: prepare, don’t memorise

Honhaar Editorial · Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

An interview means your paper application worked. Now the panel wants to confirm that the person matches the essay — and see how you think under mild pressure.

The STAR method

For any “tell us about a time…” question, answer in four beats: Situation (one sentence of context), Task (what you had to achieve), Action (what you did — the longest part), Result (a number or concrete outcome, plus what you learned). Keep answers to 60–120 seconds.

15 common questions

  1. Tell us about yourself. (90 seconds: background, defining experience, goal.)
  2. Why do you deserve this scholarship?
  3. Why this course and this university?
  4. What will you do after you graduate?
  5. Describe a time you led a team.
  6. Describe a failure and what you learned.
  7. What problem in your community would you solve first, and how?
  8. How will you contribute to the scholar network?
  9. What is your biggest weakness?
  10. Tell us about a recent development in your field.
  11. How do you handle conflict?
  12. Who influenced you most, and why?
  13. If you don’t get this scholarship, what will you do?
  14. How will you give back?
  15. Do you have any questions for us?

Prepare stories, not scripts

Write down six real stories — leadership, failure, teamwork, initiative, a hard decision, community impact. Most questions can be answered with one of them. Practise telling each aloud in under two minutes. Memorised paragraphs sound robotic; prepared stories sound confident.

Online interview setup

  • Test your connection, camera and microphone the day before on the same platform.
  • Camera at eye level, light in front of you, plain background.
  • Keep a single page of notes off-camera — keywords only.
  • Close every other app and silence your phone.
  • Join five minutes early.

The day before

  • Re-read your entire application — they will ask about it.
  • Read the scholarship’s mission and recent news.
  • Prepare two thoughtful questions for the panel.
  • Lay out clothes and documents; sleep early.

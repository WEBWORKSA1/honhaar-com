Education loans explained, without the jargon

Honhaar Editorial · Updated October 2026 · 9 min read

A loan should be your last funding layer, after scholarships, assistantships and family savings. When you do borrow, three numbers decide what it really costs: the interest rate, how interest behaves during your course, and how long you take to repay.

The funding stack — in order

  1. Scholarships and fee waivers (free money).
  2. Assistantships, campus jobs and paid internships.
  3. Family savings and support.
  4. Education loan for the remaining gap — and only that gap.

Who lends

Public-sector banks often offer the lowest rates and may follow government model schemes, but can be slower and stricter on paperwork. Private banks move faster and may lend more without collateral to strong profiles. NBFCs and specialist lenders focus on study-abroad loans, approve quickly and may lend unsecured against your admission and future earning potential — usually at higher rates. International lenders may lend to students at certain universities without an Indian co-applicant.

The three costs that matter

1. Interest rate — fixed or floating

Most Indian education loans are floating, linked to a benchmark rate. A 1-percentage-point difference on a ₹20 lakh loan over 10 years changes your total interest by well over a lakh. Always compare the effective rate including processing fees.

2. Moratorium interest

The moratorium is the course period plus a grace period (often 6–12 months) when you don’t pay EMIs. But interest usually keeps running. Some lenders charge simple interest during this time; others compound it. On a two-year master’s, that can add a large sum to your principal before your first EMI. Paying just the interest during your course — even partly — is one of the smartest moves you can make.

3. Tenure

Longer tenure lowers your EMI but raises total interest. Choose the shortest tenure whose EMI fits comfortably within your realistic starting salary, then prepay when you can — check that the lender doesn’t charge prepayment penalties.

Documents you’ll usually need

  • Admission letter and fee structure
  • Academic records and test scores
  • KYC for student and co-applicant
  • Co-applicant income proof (salary slips, ITRs, bank statements)
  • Collateral papers, if secured
  • Cost-of-attendance estimate for study abroad

Tax benefit in India

Under Section 80E of the Income Tax Act, interest paid on a qualifying education loan from an approved lender can be deducted from taxable income for up to eight years, with no upper limit on the interest amount. Check current rules and whether your tax regime allows it with a tax professional.

Smart borrowing checklist

  • Get quotes from at least three lenders in the same week.
  • Ask: simple or compound interest during moratorium?
  • Ask about processing fee, prepayment charges and insurance add-ons.
  • Borrow only the gap after scholarships.
  • Set up auto-pay of interest during the course if your family can.

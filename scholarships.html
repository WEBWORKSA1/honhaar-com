How to use this finder well

Most students search for scholarships once, find a handful of famous names and stop. Winners do the opposite: they build a long list of 15–25 programmes, cut it to the 6–10 where they clearly meet every eligibility line, then apply to all of them with a reusable set of documents. A single fully funded award can be worth more than a decade of part-time work, so the hours you invest here have the highest return of anything you will do this year.

Read eligibility like a selector

Before you fall in love with a scholarship, check four things on the official page: citizenship or domicile, the level and subject you must be enrolled in, any income ceiling, and minimum marks. If you fail even one “must”, move on — selectors rarely make exceptions, and a rejected application costs you time you could spend on a programme you can win.

Domestic first, then international

Government schemes in India and Pakistan reach far more students than any international award, but they reward the organised: correct certificates, bank accounts linked to your ID, and verification by your institute before the cut-off. International awards like Chevening, Commonwealth or MEXT are fewer and more competitive; they reward a clear story, leadership evidence and strong references. Apply to both kinds in parallel.

Never pay to apply

Legitimate scholarships do not charge an application fee to “unlock” money, and no agent can guarantee selection. Read our guide on spotting scholarship scams before you share documents with anyone.
